# Photo Breakthrough Stained Glass

Transform a supplied photograph into a photo-led stained-glass artwork. Preserve the original subject and its important photographic qualities; use stained glass as a structural intervention around and behind the subject, not as an automatic full-image repaint.

## Required workflow

1. Inspect the source photo and identify the primary subject, its silhouette, overlap, direction, lighting, and the quiet background areas.
2. Decide the fidelity split. Default to roughly 60% truthful photography and 40% stained-glass intervention unless the user requests a stronger glass treatment. Keep the primary subject more photographic than the surrounding field.
3. Build a vertical 3:5 composition unless another ratio is requested. Use a deep charcoal or near-black window/background field. Never add paper, parchment, beige paper, paper fibers, scan grain, or torn-paper texture.
4. Translate secondary forms into translucent, source-derived glass panes. Use thin dark lead came, irregular pane shapes, subtle internal color variation, and restrained light transmission. Avoid glossy plastic or 3D rendering.
5. Create the breakthrough event. The subject must appear to push through the glass: open the panes around the subject, use jagged displaced shards, broken or interrupted lead segments, fine radiating cracks, and a few physically plausible floating fragments. Do not draw a continuous heavy black outline around the subject.
6. Increase glass area around the subject when requested, but keep the subject's natural photographic edges, texture, and overlap visible. Glass should frame, support, and fracture around the subject rather than replace its identity.
7. If text is requested or appropriate, use one short line only. Render it as small translucent glass segments joined by fine lead lines. Keep it legible and subordinate; do not use ordinary ink or typed text.
8. Generate with the supplied photo as the primary reference. Inspect at normal and thumbnail scale. If the subject became too glass-like, regenerate once with the targeted correction: restore the original photographic subject and reduce glass intervention on the subject itself.

## Material and composition rules

- Preserve the source-specific subject: real leaf contours, flower petals, animal silhouette, vehicle proportions, face, architecture, or other defining details.
- Derive pane shapes from visible source geometry: veins, petals, branches, wave rhythms, car panels, shadows, bokeh circles, or horizon lines.
- Use one dominant source-derived chromatic structure when useful; keep other hues subordinate and source-aware.
- Prefer irregular broken boundaries over uniform frames. Let crack direction and shard displacement respond to the subject's movement or point of impact.
- Keep the photographic opening recognizable. The glass can be stronger in the background and around the perimeter than over the defining subject.
- Use lead lines selectively. They may stop, bend, separate, or disappear where the subject breaks through. They must not become fake black contour lines around every leaf, petal, feather, or object edge.
- Keep glass fragments translucent and tactile, with slight pigment variation and restrained glow. Avoid neon, glossy highlights, plastic, cinematic lighting, religious symbols, cathedral architecture, logos, watermarks, and extra decorative objects.

## Breakthrough correction checklist

Before returning, verify:

- The source subject is still immediately recognizable.
- The primary subject retains substantial original photographic texture and natural edges.
- The glass area is visible and more extensive around the subject, but does not erase it.
- The subject visibly breaks the glass plane through displaced shards and interrupted lead lines.
- Cracks radiate from plausible impact points and do not form a uniform decorative border.
- There are no thick artificial black outlines around the subject.
- There is no paper texture of any kind.
- Any lettering is exact, legible, made from glass pieces, and subordinate.
- The final image is flat artwork, not a glossy 3D render or mockup.

## Output

Return the generated image and one brief Chinese rationale describing how the original subject was preserved and how the breakthrough glass effect was built. Do not reveal the full prompt unless the user asks.
